How Purely scores food

Not "is it healthy?" but "how close is this to the pure, real thing?" An apple is 100. Butter (cream + salt) is ~99. A tin of tomatoes is ~94 — great, but it isn't a tomato. Then it slides down as factory steps pile up.

90+ Pure — whole food, as nature made it
70+ Lightly processed — real food, a little help
45+ Processed — real food plus refined bits
20+ Ultra-processed — more factory than kitchen
0+ Avoid — heavily ultra-processed

What costs points

Nothing for whole foods: meat, fish, milk, eggs, fruit, veg, grains, nuts, herbs, spices, salt, water, coffee, tea.

A little for traditional steps: vinegar, cheese, yeast, cultures, olive oil, juice, purée.

More for refined ingredients: white flour, sugar, seed oils, palm fat, starch, milk powders.

A lot for ultra-processed markers: dextrose, glucose syrups, maltodextrin, flavourings, yeast extract, modified starch, protein isolates, hydrogenated fats.

Most for cosmetic additives: artificial colours, sweeteners, flavour enhancers, industrial emulsifiers.

Instant near-zero for nitrites and nitrates in cured meat.

Plus

Ingredients listed first count for more (they make up more of the product). Each extra ingredient takes off a little. And several ultra-processed markers together take off extra, because together they mean a formulated product rather than a recipe.

Unlike Yuka, a long list of real ingredients still scores well, and a single refined ingredient doesn't get a free pass.
